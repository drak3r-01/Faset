# Lot 6.7 - Art direction (internal lot)

Not in the reference plan. Opened after the playable client (6.6): a first test of the Windows build found the graphics weak (flat grey-white surfaces, one grey box as the weapon in hand, plain blocks as characters). The target is an original low-poly and pixel look, bold and saturated, in the spirit of fast browser shooters but with no asset, name or map copied from any of them.

- **Model/effort:** Opus, high | **Depends on:** 2.3-2.6, 2.13, 2.15, 6.6 | **Branch:** `claude/project-thread-fv86de` (one PR)
- **Owns:** `render` data and shaders (materials, ambience profiles), `assets/weapons`, `client` weapon models. **Additive changes allowed in:** `mapfmt` (material names), `assets` (recipe patterns), `editor` (material list).

## Goal

Every surface of the game looks like hand-made pixel art, every weapon is a recognisable detailed model, the weapon in hand is built from it, and the light, the sky and the fog are bold. No image is stored or generated by an image tool: every texel comes from code.

## What was done

1. **Procedural pixel textures** (`crates/shaders/src/pixel.wgsl`): 14 new material patterns (ids 14 to 27) evaluated per fragment from integer texel coordinates, 16 texels per material cell, nearest filtering (no interpolation), triplanar with the dominant plane taking all the weight. Each pattern returns a blend between the material's two colours and a multiplicative shade quantised in a few steps: brick, wood boards, grass, sand, cobblestone, concrete panels, riveted metal, animated water, tiles, dirt, snow, crate, hazard stripes, foliage. Texels smaller than a screen pixel fade to the pattern's mean so distant surfaces do not shimmer (`distant_patterns_do_not_shimmer` covers them).
2. **Material table** (`render/data/materials.ron`): ids 14 to 27 are one default per pattern, ids 28 to 49 are colour variants (sand brick, pale and dark wood, sandstone, mossy stone, orange, blue, red and green painted metal, savanna grass, lagoon and deep water, white sand, basalt, teal tiles, asphalt, palm leaves, ice, slate brick, whitewashed boards, red clay). `Pattern::ALL` lists the 28 patterns (the recipe `Pattern` of `faset-assets` too).
3. **Map materials** (`mapfmt::geometry::MATERIAL_NAMES`): maps keep naming materials as strings; `brick`, `wood`, `sand`, `concrete`, `metal`, `tiles`, `snow`, `water`, `hazard` now paint the pixel patterns, and the new names above are available (the editor lists them). `checker`, `grid`, `lava`, `emissive` and `hologram` keep their classic look.
4. **Weapon models** (`assets/weapons/*.ron`): the 12 weapons of the game are 200 to 420 triangle box models (assault rifle, SMG, pump shotgun, precision rifle with scope and bipod, sidearm, baguette, toaster, banana peel, bubble gun, confetti cannon, rubber chicken launcher, rocket launcher), muzzle towards -Z, grip at the origin, with pixel metal and wood. They stay within the asset-tool weapon budget (`asset-tool validate assets/weapons`).
5. **Weapon in hand and carried weapons** (`client::weapon_models`, `client::scene`, `client::preview`): the recipes are embedded, built with `build_mesh`, uploaded once; the first-person view draws the model of the equipped weapon with a right hand on the grip and a left hand on the fore-end (springs unchanged); other players and the loadout preview carry the models (primary in hand, secondary on the hip, melee on the back, gadget on the left hip) and gained boots, belt, backpack and visor. A weapon without a recipe falls back to a box.
6. **Light, sky and fog** (`render/data/ambience`): `day`, `sunset`, `desert` and `snow` are brighter and more saturated (sun intensity, sky ambient, vivid zenith and horizon, less haze, saturation 1.2 to 1.3, a slightly lower bloom threshold). Night, neon, storm, space and underwater keep their mood. The nine profile references, the sky, shadow, weather and post references and the other captures that use `day` were regenerated.

## Decisions

- **Shader-side textures, no texture files:** a CPU texture generator was not needed; the patterns are exact integer functions, identical on every GPU, and cost no memory.
- **Tint through the vertex colour:** a map brush is `(material id, tint)`; new names pick a colour variant instead of tinting, so tints stay white.
- **Palette names** of weapon recipes use the default theme; pixel patterns are tinted with `white` so they keep the table colour.
- **Characters** stay box-built (no new mesh format); they gained only details.

## Remaining gaps

- The weapon in hand is still a normal instance (it can sink into a wall: no first-person depth pass).
- Characters are not animated and have no pixel materials.

## Acceptance

`cargo xtask ci` is green in Docker. `pixel_swatch_matches_reference` (all 14 pixel patterns), `distant_patterns_do_not_shimmer`, `weapon_models::every_embedded_recipe_builds`, `asset-tool validate assets/weapons` (13 recipes, 0 failed), the regenerated references, and `match_scene` (a live match with the model in hand) pass.
